How the fuel margins work

Every fuel and time figure on this page runs off these settings, and the defaults are deliberately pessimistic. Plotted distances get a 15% track allowance for harbour manoeuvring, dodging traffic, standing off headlands and wandering off the ideal line. Burn gets a further 10% for headwinds, foul tide, a fouled hull and model error. Each stop costs 3 L of 5-knot running and repositioning, 5 L where you'll be spearing and picking up divers. The first 10 L in the tank is treated as unusable, and a third of the rest is held back, so every route must finish its legs with 45 L or more on the gauge.

All trips at a glance

Engine curve

Platform: Desner Sport 580, 5.8 m LOA, 25° deadrise, 115 L integrated tank. Factory weights: hull 605 kg, console 40 kg, seat 30 kg, rollbar 15 kg, T-top 30 kg, so 720 kg as rigged. Add the Mercury 115 Pro XS at about 165 kg and she is roughly 885 kg dry, about 1,150 kg with a full tank, two aboard and day gear.

Rules of engagement

Three overlapping rule sets now shape where you can fish and anchor: the 2025 Hauraki Gulf protection areas, Northland's rāhui tapu no-take areas, and exotic caulerpa controls. All three are drawn on the map as rough footprints. Load the official boundary files into the GO9 for the real lines.

Where you can't shoot

Twelve Hauraki Gulf High Protection Areas ban recreational fishing of any kind, spearfishing included: Mokohīnau Islands, Te Hauturu-o-Toi / Little Barrier, Tiritiri Matangi, The Noises, Rangitoto and Motutapu, Kawau Bay, Pakatoa and Tarahiki / Shag Island, Motukawao Islands, Cape Colville, Slipper Island / Whakahau, and the Aldermen Islands north and south. Marine reserves (Goat Island, Tāwharanui, Long Bay–Okura, Te Matuku, Cathedral Cove, Poor Knights, Tūhua's north coast) are no-take too.

In Northland, rāhui tapu areas close Maunganui Bay (Deep Water Cove) to Oke Bay and the Mimiwhangata peninsula to all fishing except kina.

Where you can't anchor

Exotic caulerpa controls: on Aotea's west coast the Broken Islands and Tryphena Harbour are red zones with no anchoring without a permit, while Port Fitzroy has a permit-free anchoring zone. Anchoring is also banned in the Ahuahu / Great Mercury and Te Rāwhiti Inlet (Bay of Islands) controlled areas. At Onetangi and Thompson's Point on Waiheke you can anchor, as long as you clean your anchor and chain before leaving and don't disturb the seabed with damaging fishing methods. Wherever you anchor, check and rinse the anchor and chain before moving on.

Standing orders

  • Thirds, conservatively: treat 10 L as unusable, then keep a third of the remaining 105 L untouched. That puts the planning floor at 45 L on the gauge, and every verdict on this page is measured against it.
  • Fuel gauges on small boats lie, especially in a seaway. Trust VesselView's fuel-used total and fill to full at every stop on a multi-day route.
  • Break-in comes first. Follow Mercury's break-in schedule in the owner's manual before running long legs at high rpm; Basalt is sized for that.
  • On a boat 6 m or under, Auckland's navigation bylaw expects lifejackets worn underway. Check the current wording before your first run.
  • 5 knots within 200 m of shore or any vessel flying a dive flag, and within 50 m of swimmers. Fly Flag A whenever someone's in the water spearing.
  • The Bay of Islands is a marine mammal sanctuary: no swimming within 300 m of marine mammals, and slow down around them.
  • Pest-free islands (Tiritiri, Motuora, Rotoroa, Rakitu, Tūhua and more) want your boat and bags checked for rodents, insects and seeds before you land.
  • Log a trip report with Coastguard (VHF or the Coastguard app) for anything beyond the inner gulf, and carry two waterproof ways to call for help.
  • Coromandel bar crossings at Tairua and Whangamatā: cross on the incoming tide and get local advice first.
  • Rock jumps: check the depth at that state of tide, look for submerged rock, and never jump blind or alone.
  • Catch limits change; check MPI's NZ Fishing Rules app before you shoot anything.

Planning tool, not for navigation. Routes are drawn around a simplified OpenStreetMap coastline at roughly 100 m detail, keeping a short stand-off from shore; they don't know about rocks, reefs, depth or traffic lanes. Shelter ratings come from straight-line fetch to the nearest land in the wind's direction and ignore swell wrapping round headlands. Plot real routes on the Simrad with C-MAP, and confirm fuel hours, bookings, landing permissions and protected-area boundaries before each trip.
